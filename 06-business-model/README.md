## 1. The model in one page

**The business is: basis points on a very large volume, plus a share of the finance it enables, plus
programme contracts — with a data asset that compounds.**

| Revenue stream | Basis | Illustrative rate | Starts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketplace commission | % of GMV | 1–3% | Phase 1 |
| Payment / escrow share | % of settled value | 0.3–0.8% | Phase 1 |
| Embedded finance | % of principal (origination + servicing) | 1–3% | Phase 2 |
| Input commerce | Margin or listing fee | 3–8% | Phase 2 |
| Data and analytics | Subscription / per report | Fixed | Phase 2 |
| Government / NGO programmes | Per beneficiary or contract | Fixed | Phase 1 |

**What the platform does not do:** charge farmers for basic advice; depend on card rails; take
balance-sheet credit risk in Phase 1–2; sell advertising as a primary line.

## 2. The economics, in one table (illustrative ⚪)

At **ETB 40 bn GMV** (Year 5, ~3–5% of the cereal market):

| Line | Base | Rate | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketplace commission | ETB 40 bn GMV | 1.5% | ETB 600 m |
| Payment / escrow share | ETB 40 bn settled | 0.3% | ETB 120 m |
| Embedded finance | ETB 3.0 bn origination | 2.0% | ETB 60 m |
| Input commerce | ETB 4.0 bn input sales | 4.0% | ETB 160 m |
| Data and analytics | ~40 institutional clients | ~ETB 150k avg | ETB 6 m |
| Programmes | 500,000 beneficiaries | ETB 80/beneficiary | ETB 40 m |
| **Total** | | | **~ETB 986 m (~US$6.1 m)** |

**Reality check:** US$6 m of revenue on a national platform serving 750,000 farmers is a *thin*
business in dollar terms — because Ethiopia's economy is US$210 bn and farm-gate prices are low. This
is the honest picture, and it is why the model must eventually expand beyond cereals (livestock,
horticulture, coffee premiums, export traceability) and beyond fees (programme delivery at scale).

**Where the upside actually is:**
- Coffee, horticulture and livestock (higher per-unit values → higher absolute fees).
- Export traceability (EUDR) as a paid compliance service.
- Programme delivery (government and donors pay per beneficiary, not per transaction).
- Finance at scale (ETB 2.58 trn of unmet demand — 1% of that is ETB 25.8 bn of origination).

## 3. Strategic position in the value chain

```
FARMER ──► AGGREGATOR/AGENT ──► PLATFORM ──► BUYER/PROCESSOR ──► EXPORTER ──► WORLD
   ▲                                  ▲
   │                                  │
   └──── credit, advice, inputs ──────┘
```

The platform sits at the **information and settlement point**, not on inventory. It earns from the
transaction and from the services attached to it, and never from holding the commodity (except as a
deliberate, limited Phase 1 tactic).

**Why this position:** it is capital-light, it captures data from both sides, and it is where the
intermediary's spread currently sits. The intermediary survives because it provides credit, logistics
and immediacy together. The platform must match that bundle to take the position.

## 4. Three scenarios

| Scenario | Assumptions | Year 5 GMV | Year 5 revenue | Viability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | 2 regions, cereals only, slow lender uptake | ETB 8 bn | ETB ~200 m | Break-even at scale; small |
| **Base** | 5 regions, 3 commodities, 2 lender partners | ETB 40 bn | ETB ~990 m | Profitable; regional significance |
| **Stretch** | 8 regions, 6 commodities, exports, national programme contracts | ETB 100 bn | ETB ~2.6 bn | National infrastructure; strategic asset |

## 5. What has to be true for the model to work

| Assumption | Threshold | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Farmers repeat | ≥40% second-season | Business is acquisition-dependent; never profitable |
| Take rate sustainable | ≥1.2% blended | Cannot cover agent + support cost |
| Agent cost | ≤30% of gross revenue | Agent network becomes uneconomic |
| Cost to serve a transaction | Falling 20%/yr | Scale does not improve margin |
| Lender participation | ≥2 partners by month 15 | Finance revenue stays near zero |
| Programme contracts | ≥1 by month 12 | Revenue too thin to fund operations |
| Birr stability | No >30% annual depreciation | USD cost lines destroy margin |

## 6. The honest risks to the business model

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Thin market** | Revenue grows slower than cost | Programme contracts; higher-value commodities |
| **Side-selling** | GMV never materialises | Escrow, credit linkage, guarantee |
| **Price controls** | Margins capped or suspended | Commodity flexibility; programme revenue |
| **Incumbent entry** | State platform captures the market | Partner posture; cooperative relationships |
| **FX depreciation** | USD costs balloon | Local costs; local hosting; minimal imports |
| **Conflict** | Regional shutdown | Diversification; light assets |
| **Agent churn** | Distribution collapses | Bundled income; device support |

---

## 7. What the model becomes at maturity

At Phase 4–5, the platform's economics shift from **transaction fees** to **infrastructure rent**:

| Phase | Dominant revenue | Margin profile |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Programme contracts + small marketplace fees | Negative |
| 2 | Marketplace + finance + inputs | Break-even |
| 3 | Marketplace + finance + logistics | Profitable |
| 4 | API/partner revenue + data + programme | Infrastructure-like |

That trajectory — from subsidy-funded operations to infrastructure rent — is the same path Estonia's
X-Road, India's UPI and Kenya's M-Pesa followed. It takes 8–12 years, not 3.
