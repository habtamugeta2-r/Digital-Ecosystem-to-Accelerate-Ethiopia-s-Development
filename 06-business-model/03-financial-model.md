## 3. Cumulative funding need

| Year | EBITDA | Capex | Working capital | Net cash need | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (29.5) | 4.0 | 2.0 | (35.5) | (35.5) |
| 2 | (31.0) | 6.0 | 8.0 | (45.0) | (80.5) |
| 3 | 83.8 | 8.0 | 22.0 | 53.8 | (26.7) |
| 4 | 540.8 | 10.0 | 45.0 | 485.8 | 459.1 |
| 5 | 1,433.2 | 12.0 | 60.0 | 1,361.2 | 1,820.3 |

**Peak funding requirement: ~ETB 81 m (≈US$0.5 m).**

That is a remarkably small number for a national platform — which is both the opportunity and the
warning. It is small because the model is asset-light and agent-led. It is a warning because **at that
scale the platform is fragile**: one failed season, one delayed programme payment, or one lender
withdrawal could exhaust the runway.

**Prudent approach: raise ETB 150–200 m (US$0.9–1.3 m)** — roughly 2× the modelled peak — to survive
the variance. Under-raising is the most common cause of death for platforms like this.

The year-1 shape is a **seasonal spike**: Ethiopian agriculture has two main harvests (Meher Oct–Jan,
Belg Mar–May), so monthly volumes are lumpy. Cash planning must account for this — a platform that
sizes its team for the harvest peak will be over-staffed for eight months and under-staffed for four.

**Practical implication:** use fixed-term harvest staff and agent-based delivery to flex capacity.

## 6. What breaks the model

| Shock | Revenue impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Birr depreciation >40% | USD-denominated costs (cloud, SMS) rise ~40% | Local hosting; renegotiate SMS; cap foreign spend |
| One region suspended (conflict) | −15 to −25% of GMV | Multi-region; light assets |
| Major buyer defaults | Guarantee fund drawdown | Escrow; buyer scoring; limits |
| Lender withdraws | Finance revenue → 0 | ≥3 partners; MFI first |
| State platform launched | Commission compressed | Partner; differentiate on finance and traceability |
| Commodity export ban | Coffee/export lines vanish | Domestic staples as core |
| Inflation >25% | Fixed fees erode | Index fees to CPI; review quarterly |

## 7. Key model assumptions to validate in the pilot

| Assumption | How to test | Fail threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5% take rate is acceptable | Run two price points in two cooperatives | >25% volume loss |
| 3.2 transactions/farmer/year | Track cohort in pilot | <2.0 |
| Agent can earn ETB 50k/yr | Measure agent income monthly | <ETB 30k |
| Input attach rate 25% by Y3 | Test in Year 1 pilot | <15% |
| Lender will lend on 1-season data | Ask 3 lenders in writing | All decline |
| Guarantee provision 0.3% is enough | Track actual dispute losses | >0.6% |
| Programme contracts at ETB 100/beneficiary | Negotiate 1 contract | Offer <ETB 40 |

## 8. Investment thesis, stated plainly

**What the investor is buying:**

| Asset | Value |
|---|---|
| First mover in a 30+ Mt cereal market with no digital platform | Optionality |
| A verified transaction ledger that no one else will have | Structural moat |
| Government relationships via programmes | Revenue + protection |
| A path to becoming the agricultural data and credit infrastructure | Platform upside |
| A small capital requirement (US$1–2 m to break-even) | Capital efficiency |

**What the investor is risking:**

| Risk | Probability |
|---|---|
| Side-selling prevents GMV formation | 30% |
| Conflict disrupts operations | 35% (over 5 yrs) |
| State platform crowds out | 20% |
| Thin margins never fund the team | 25% |
| Key-person/management failure | 20% |

**The investment case rests on one claim:** *in a market where no one can verify smallholder
production or repayment, whoever builds the ledger first owns credit, and credit is where the margin
is.* Every other line is supporting infrastructure for that claim.
