## 1. Poverty: a reversal, not a trajectory

Ethiopia was, for two decades, one of Africa's more credible poverty-reduction stories. That
reversed.

| Indicator | Value | Year | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poverty headcount (US$3.00/day, 2021 PPP) | **43%** (projected) | 2025 | 🟡 |
| Same measure, historical | 33% (2016), 39% (2021) | — | 🟡 |
| National poverty line poverty rate | ~69% reported in some assessments; ~37% at US$2.15/day | 2024 | 🟠 definition-dependent |
| Rural population share of the poor | ~three-quarters of the population | 2025 | 🟢 |
| Access inequality (top vs bottom quintile) | sanitation and electricity 3–4× more prevalent in top 20% | 2025 | 🟡 |
| Adults without a financial account | 39.9 million | 2024 | 🟢 |

**Causes of the reversal**, per World Bank analysis: the July 2024 exchange-rate reform and
associated inflation; removal of fuel subsidies; electricity tariff adjustments; conflict and
displacement; and drought. **The drivers are largely policy and shock, not collapse in production** —
which is important, because it means households are being squeezed between *input costs and
consumer prices*, and any intervention that improves the prices farmers receive or reduces their
cost of inputs has an outsized effect.

> ⚠️ Note on the "69% poor" figure that circulates: it uses a different (national) line and
> methodology and is not comparable to the US$3/day series. Cite the 43% figure with its line
> explicitly.

## 2. Food security

| Indicator | Value | Period | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| People in acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) | **15–16 million** | mid-2026 projection | 🟡 |
| People in need of food assistance (Eastern Africa aggregate, incl. Ethiopia) | 24.5–25.9 m | mid-2026 | 🟡 |
| WFP caseload | >10 m facing hunger; 3 m displaced | 2025 | 🟡 |
| Pregnant/breastfeeding women and children needing malnutrition treatment | 4.4 m | 2025 | 🟡 |
| Child wasting above 15% emergency threshold | parts of Somali, Oromia, Tigray, Afar | 2025 | 🟡 |
| WFP funding shortfall | US$222 m (Apr–Sep 2025) | 2025 | 🟡 |

**The drivers** are a compound of conflict, drought/flood cycles, displacement, price inflation, and
a sharp contraction in international humanitarian funding (particularly the 2025 US aid
restructuring). The 2023 food-aid diversion scandal also forced a nationwide pause in deliveries,
damaging trust in in-kind distribution — one reason **digital targeting and cash-based transfer**
have gained policy traction.

**Why this matters for a market platform.** Food insecurity in Ethiopia is not primarily a
*production* failure at the national level — the country produces 30–33 Mt of cereals — it is a
**access, distribution and loss** failure. 15–25% post-harvest loss and fragmented regional markets
mean surplus areas and deficit areas coexist within the same country. A platform that moves grain
from surplus to deficit efficiently is a food-security instrument, not just a commercial one. This is
the strongest political argument for the project.

## 3. Nutrition

| Indicator | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Stunting (children under 5) | ~35–39% (varies by source/year) | 🟠 |
| Wasting | above 15% emergency threshold in several regions | 🟡 |
| Anaemia in women of reproductive age | high (40%+ in many surveys) | 🟠 |
| Diet diversity | low; cereal-dominant diets | 🟡 |

The nutrition consequence of a cereal-heavy, low-diversity food system is a **market design problem**:
pulses, vegetables, fruit, dairy, eggs and meat are nutritious but perishable, thinly traded and
poorly financed. Horticulture loses 30–50% post-harvest. A platform that reduces perishable loss and
improves cold-chain coordination has a direct nutrition pathway, not only an income pathway.

## 4. Education

| Indicator | Value | Year | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning poverty (10-year-olds who cannot read a simple text) | **90%** | 2022–2025 | 🟢 |
| Net primary enrolment | ~89% | 2024 | 🟡 |
| Grade 12 students failing to reach 50% | 97% | 2024 | 🟡 |
| Education spend | ~3.7% of GDP (vs UNESCO 6% benchmark) | 2025 | 🟡 |
| Children out of school | ~3.6 million (conflict-affected regions) | 2024 | 🟡 |
| Primary completion | 69% boys / 65% girls | 2021 | 🟡 |

**The design implication is blunt:** a product that requires reading a screen will exclude most of
its intended users. The 8028 hotline exists precisely because voice works where text does not.

## 5. Health

| Indicator | Value | Year | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| UHC service coverage index (national) | ~39–46% | 2021–2024 | 🟢 |
| UHC SCI range across regions | ~23% (Somali) to ~64% (Addis Ababa) | 2024 | 🟡 |
| Projected UHC SCI 2030 | ~64.7% (vs 80% SDG target) | projection | 🟡 |
| Community-based health insurance population coverage | ~40% | 2023 | 🟠 |
| Health facilities with electricity | historically low; improved but uneven | — | 🟠 |

The WHO/World Bank UHC index of 33–46 places Ethiopia among the lowest-coverage countries globally.
Out-of-pocket expenditure is a major cause of distress asset sales — which is one more reason a
household that gains 10% on its crop income converts that gain into resilience rather than
consumption.

## 6. Employment and youth

| Indicator | Value | Year | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unemployment (national, ILO modelled) | 3.3–3.4% | 2024–25 | 🟢 |
| Urban unemployment | 18.9% | 2022 | 🟡 |
| Urban youth unemployment (15–29) | **27.2%** | 2022 | 🟡 |
| Youth unemployment, women vs men (15–29) | 24.9% vs 10.1% | 2024 | 🟡 |
| Population under 30 | ~70% | 2025 | 🟡 |

The headline unemployment rate (3.4%) is **misleading** because it counts subsistence farming as
employment. The meaningful numbers are urban youth unemployment (~27%) and the gender gap, which is
roughly 2.5:1 against young women. With ~70% of the population under 30, Ethiopia adds roughly
2 million people to the working-age population each year. **Agriculture cannot absorb them; the
platform economy can absorb some as agents, riders, graders, bookkeepers and aggregators** — a
labour-market argument for the project that is often overlooked.

## 7. Why the human-capital picture is a product requirement, not a caveat

| Constraint | Product response |
|---|---|
| 90% learning poverty | Voice-first IVR; icon-driven UI; no text-only flows |
| 80+ languages; 5 supported by 8028 | Launch in 5 languages minimum (Amharic, Afaan Oromo, Tigrinya, Somali, Afar) |
| Low numeracy | Agent-assisted transactions; spoken price quotes; visual price boards |
| 21.7% internet penetration | USSD and SMS as first-class channels, not fallbacks |
| 44% Tier-1 electricity | Battery-tolerant agent devices; offline-first field apps |
| Gender gap in phone ownership and accounts | Female agent recruitment targets; women-only savings groups; explicit design review |
| Distrust after aid diversion and conflict | Transparent ledgers, receipts via SMS, published price data, verified seller marks |

## Key takeaways

1. Poverty rose to 43% (US$3/day) in 2025 — a reversal driven by reform-related inflation, conflict
   and drought, not by a production collapse.
2. 15–16 million people are acutely food insecure in a country that grows 30–33 Mt of cereals: the
   failure is **distribution, loss and access**, which is addressable by market infrastructure.
3. Education and health outcomes imply a user base that cannot be assumed literate — voice and
   agents are core product, not accessibility add-ons.
4. Urban youth unemployment (~27%, and far worse for young women) creates both a social problem and
   a ready labour pool for agent, logistics and aggregation roles.
5. Nutrition argues for extending beyond cereals into pulses, horticulture and livestock — where the
   losses are highest and the market is thinnest.
