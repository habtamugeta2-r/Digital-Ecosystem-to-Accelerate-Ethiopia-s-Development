# 03.7 · Other African Comparators

*Part of [03 · International models](README.md)*

---

## 1. Nigeria: BVN and NIN

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Bank Verification Number (BVN)** | Biometric identity for bank customers; ~60 million+ enrolments; makes bank-to-bank KYC portable |
| **National Identification Number (NIN)** | Government identity; linking to SIM and bank accounts mandated (with repeated deadlines and disruptions) |
| **Effect** | BVN de-duplicated the banking system and enabled credit bureau development; NIN-SIM linkage reduced fraud but excluded millions when enforced abruptly |
| **Lesson** | A banking-sector identity standard can precede a national ID and deliver real value — but **enforcement deadlines without enrolment capacity exclude people.** Ethiopia's Fayda mandate (bank accounts by 31 Dec 2026) faces exactly this risk |

**Direct warning for Ethiopia:** the Fayda→bank-account mandate is the right policy but the sequencing
matters. With ~53.4 m registered out of ~90 m target, and lower coverage in Gambella (~28,300) and
pastoral regions, a hard deadline without a grace mechanism and a non-biometric fallback will lock
people out of their own money.

## 2. Ghana: mobile money interoperability and ID

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mobile money interoperability | Achieved 2018 — the first in Africa; Ghana Interbank Payment and Settlement Systems (GhIPSS) |
| Effect | Cross-wallet and wallet-to-bank transfers became routine; merchant payments grew |
| Identity | Ghana Card; biometric national ID with mandatory SIM registration |
| Lesson | **Interoperability is a policy decision, not a technology one** — and once enforced, volumes grow fast |

**Ethiopia parallel:** EthSwitch's mandated interoperable QR and M-Pesa's October 2025 integration are
the Ethiopian version. The lesson is to **complete the mandate** — partial interoperability leaves
value on the table.

---

## 3. Tanzania: mobile money as the primary financial system

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mobile money accounts | ~50 million+; the majority of adults |
| Agent networks | Dense, rural, profitable |
| Agriculture | Digitised input vouchers (the "Tanzania Agriculture Input Voucher" model) |
| Lesson | Where banking infrastructure is thin, mobile money **becomes** the financial system, not an adjunct. Voucher digitisation in agriculture works when the mobile money layer is dense |

**Ethiopia parallel:** telebirr is on the same path, but Ethiopia's account-ownership (49%) and
activation (16% 30-day active) lag Tanzania. The difference is the agent economics and rural density.

## 4. Rwanda: beyond Irembo

| Programme | Detail | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| e-Soko | Agricultural market prices by phone | Price information alone produces modest gains |
| Land tenure regularisation | Systematic land registration and titling | Titling unlocks collateral — the thing Ethiopia structurally lacks |
| Umurenge SACCOs | Village-level savings cooperatives digitised | Group savings + digital rails is a powerful combination for the unbanked |
| Digital ID + Irembo integration | Services require ID | Identity as the gateway to services |

**Ethiopia parallel:** Ethiopia has ~30,000 cooperatives that play a similar role to SACCOs but are
weaker and less digitised. **Digitising cooperative savings and records is one of the cheapest,
highest-trust entry points available.**

---

## 5. Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana: "platform cooperatives"

A newer model in West Africa: agricultural cooperatives that own or contract a digital platform, with
member equity and shared surplus. It solves the trust problem (the platform is *theirs*) and the
adoption problem (member obligation).

**Ethiopian application:** the strongest version of the proposed platform for Ethiopia may be a
**cooperative-anchored structure** — the platform is a company, but cooperatives hold equity, sit on
the board, and share in the fee revenue. This converts the platform from an extractive intermediary
into member infrastructure, which is politically and socially defensible.

## 6. Comparative table: where Ethiopia sits

| Country | ID | Mobile money | Interoperability | Agent density | Agri digital | Ethiopia's position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kenya | Mature | Very high | Yes | Very high | Moderate | Behind on all except ID |
| Rwanda | Mature | High | Yes | High | Moderate (e-Soko, Irembo) | Behind, but comparable trajectory |
| Ghana | Mature | High | Yes (2018, first) | High | Moderate | Behind on interoperability depth |
| Tanzania | Mature | Very high | Yes | Very high | High (vouchers) | Behind |
| Nigeria | Mixed (BVN strong, NIN contested) | High | Yes | High | Low | Ahead on ID integration |
| **Ethiopia** | **Scaling fast (53.4 m)** | **High nominal (60.6 m telebirr), low active (16%)** | **Recent (2025)** | **Nominal high, rural low** | **Policy-strong (DAR), execution early** | — |

**The pattern:** Ethiopia has closed the gap on **identity and policy** faster than any peer, and lags
on **activation and rural depth**. That is a specific, addressable gap — and it is a gap that an
agricultural platform is unusually well suited to close, because agriculture is where rural activation
actually happens.

## 7. The regional integration vector

| Framework | Status | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **AfCFTA** | Kenya–Ethiopia MoU signed on cross-border trade facilitation | Export market access for processed agricultural goods |
| **PAPSS** | Ethiopian non-bank mobile money operators preparing integration (Letter of No Objection from NBE required) | Cross-border payments for traders; an Addis→Lagos payment in ~120 seconds is the stated ambition |
| **Ethiopia–Djibouti** | Single corridor, ~90–95% of trade | Concentration risk |
| **Berbera corridor** | Missing transit agreement is the binding constraint | Diversification option |
| **EUDR (EU deforestation regulation)** | Compliance required for coffee, cocoa, etc. | **Traceability is now mandatory for market access** — a direct, paid use case for the platform |

**The EUDR point deserves emphasis.** Ethiopian coffee exports to the EU will increasingly require
plot-level geolocation and deforestation-free proof. That is a **regulated, non-discretionary demand**
for exactly the traceability data an agricultural platform produces. It is the single clearest
"someone will pay for this" use case in the entire dossier.

---

## Key takeaways

1. **Nigeria's NIN–SIM enforcement shows the danger** of hard ID deadlines without enrolment capacity
   — directly relevant to Ethiopia's Fayda→bank mandate.
2. **Ghana proved interoperability is a policy decision**, and it can be completed in one step.
3. **Tanzania shows what a dense agent network and digitised vouchers can achieve** in agriculture.
4. **Rwanda's land titling unlocked collateral** — the structural constraint Ethiopia cannot fix with
   software.
5. **Cooperative-anchored platform ownership** (West African model) is the most politically durable
   structure for Ethiopia.
6. **EUDR traceability is the clearest paid use case**: a regulated requirement that only a
   data-collecting platform can satisfy.
