# 03.6 · Jumia and Pan-African E-Commerce: the Cautionary Tale

*Part of [03 · International models](README.md)*

> **Why study it.** Jumia is the most ambitious and best-funded attempt to build "the Amazon of
> Africa." Its history is the most complete record available of what fails, and why — in conditions
> considerably more favourable than Ethiopia's.

---

## 1. The trajectory

| Period | Event |
|---|---|
| 2012 | Founded in Nigeria |
| 2016 | Rocket Internet consolidation across Africa |
| 2019 | IPO on NYSE |
| 2019 | Exits Cameroon, Tanzania; earlier exits from Rwanda, Gabon, Congo |
| 2022 | ~20% workforce reduction; retrenchment begins |
| Dec 2023 | **Jumia Food shut down** across all markets |
| 2023 | Exits everyday grocery categories |
| Oct 2024 | **Exits South Africa (Zando) and Tunisia** — 5th and 6th market exits |
| 2023–25 | Operating losses narrowed (64% decline in 2023, to US$73 m); cash burn cut; never profitable |
| 2026 | Focused on 9 markets: Nigeria, Egypt, Kenya, Morocco, Algeria, Ghana, Ivory Coast, Senegal, Uganda |

**Years of annual losses: 11+.** Jumia has never produced a full-year profit.

---

## 2. What went wrong

| Failure mode | Detail | Generalisable lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Geographic over-expansion** | Seven countries at launch, each with different currencies, logistics, languages and regulators | Focus beats coverage |
| **Category over-expansion** | Food delivery, groceries (low margin, high spoilage), payment services, logistics-as-a-service | Every category adds operational complexity; low-margin categories destroy the P&L |
| **GMV obsession** | Growth incentivised by gross merchandise value, which hides negative contribution margin | Track contribution margin, not GMV |
| **Logistics dependency** | Third-party delivery was unreliable; Jumia had to build its own network | Control the last mile or the economics never close |
| **Cash-on-delivery dominance** | High refusal rates, returns, and cash handling cost | COD is a working-capital and fraud problem, not a convenience |
| **Low purchasing power** | Order values too small to absorb delivery cost | Unit economics are structural, not fixable by scale alone |
| **Trust deficit** | Counterfeit concerns, delivery failures, payment reluctance | Trust is the product |
| **FX volatility** | Currency devaluations repeatedly wiped out reported revenue growth (e.g. Q2 2024 revenue fell 17% reported but grew 15% constant currency) | In volatile-currency markets, revenue in USD terms can shrink while the business grows |

---

## 3. What Jumia got right (and is still true)

| Positive | Detail |
|---|---|
| Category creation | It proved Africans will buy online at scale when trust and delivery are solved |
| Logistics investment | Its own network became a genuine asset and is now offered to third parties |
| Data and credit | JumiaPay and its data became a credit origination asset |
| Narrowing to profitability | The 2023–25 retrenchment is textbook: cut categories, cut markets, cut headcount, focus |
| B2B pivot | Jumia's growing B2B wholesale business serves informal retailers — much better unit economics than consumer electronics |

**The most important lesson is the last one.** Jumia's most durable growth came from **B2B wholesale
to informal retailers**, not from consumer e-commerce. That is exactly the structure recommended here
for Ethiopia: **serve the trade, not the consumer.**

---

## 4. Ethiopian e-commerce: the same failure modes, at smaller scale

| Failure mode | Ethiopian evidence |
|---|---|
| Sub-scale revenue | 58% of ~80 surveyed e-commerce startups earn under US$10k/year; two-thirds have fewer than 10,000 users |
| Funding scarcity | ~70% of startups report annual revenue ≤US$20k (JICA); venture funding is thin |
| Logistics fragility | Fragmented last-mile; only a few operators (Eshi, Tikus, Zmall) in Addis |
| Trust | Verified-seller programmes are an active topic in local tech press because fraud is common |
| Consolidation | Qefira shut down; Buy2Go and Zmall announced a merger in 2023 explicitly to share fleets and reach agri supply chains |
| Payment constraint | 1% of adults have bought online with a digital payment |

The Buy2Go–Zmall merger is the most instructive local data point: **two Addis e-commerce platforms
merged because neither could afford its own fleet, and both immediately looked to agricultural supply
chains for volume.** That is the market telling you where the volume is.

---

## 5. The five rules that follow

1. **Narrow the geography.** One region, one cluster, one commodity — not seven countries.
2. **Narrow the category.** Bulk agricultural commodities with known grades. Not groceries, not food
   delivery, not electronics.
3. **Control delivery economics from day one**, even if you contract the trucks. Own the dispatch,
   the pricing and the reliability data.
4. **Price for contribution margin, not GMV.** A 30-tonne grain trade at ETB 750,000 with a 2% fee
   produces ETB 15,000 of gross revenue and can be profitable. A US$8 consumer order cannot.
5. **B2B before B2C.** Informal retailers, cooperatives, processors and millers are better customers
   than households: higher order values, repeat purchase, less marketing spend, lower return rates.

---

## 6. Where Jumia's experience directly contradicts the source brief

The original brief suggested a broad e-commerce marketplace as a strong opportunity and cited Jumia
as a model. That reading was reasonable when it was written, but the evidence now points the other
way: **Jumia's own strategic direction over 2023–26 has been to do less consumer e-commerce, not
more**, and to move toward B2B and logistics. The brief's own caveat — *"many broad 'Africa Amazon'
efforts struggle due to infrastructure gaps and trust issues"* — is the accurate part, and it should
be weighted more heavily than the "Jumia built pan-African e-commerce" framing.

---

## Key takeaways

1. Jumia has lost money for 11+ years and exited six markets; the failure modes are geographic
   over-expansion, category sprawl, GMV obsession and logistics dependency.
2. Its most durable business is **B2B wholesale to informal retailers** — the same structural insight
   that argues for an agricultural B2B platform in Ethiopia.
3. In volatile-currency markets, reported USD revenue can fall while the underlying business grows;
   measure in local currency.
4. Ethiopia's own e-commerce sector shows the same failure modes at smaller scale; the
   Buy2Go–Zmall merger is the market pointing at agricultural volume.
5. **Narrow, B2B, logistics-controlled, contribution-margin-positive** is the only viable path.
