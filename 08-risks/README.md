## The five that actually matter

### 1. Conflict
Ethiopia has active or recent conflict in Tigray, Amhara and Afar, with renewed fighting from late 2026.
The platform's core regions (Oromia, Amhara) are adjacent or affected. **No operational mitigation fully
addresses this** — only geographic diversification and asset-light structures.

### 2. Side-selling
Farmers accept a bid then sell to a trader who arrives with cash. This is not dishonesty — it is
rational behaviour when the trader offers immediacy. **Mitigation: escrow, credit linkage, guarantee,
and settlement speed.**

### 3. Credit default
The data may not predict repayment, or a drought may hit the whole portfolio at once. **Mitigation:
small initial cohorts, first-loss cover, geographic spread, index insurance, and a hard stop at PAR-30
>15%.**

### 4. State competition
A ministry launches "the national agricultural platform" with a mandate and a budget. **Mitigation:
partner, don't compete; cooperative equity; be the delivery mechanism.**

### 5. Agent failure
Agents cannot earn enough and churn, destroying distribution. **Mitigation: bundle three revenue
streams, concentrate territories, subsidise year 1, pick agents with existing businesses.**

## The risk posture, stated plainly

**This is a high-risk venture in a high-risk environment, and it is not appropriate for capital that
cannot be lost.** The mitigations below reduce probability and severity; they do not eliminate the
possibility that the platform fails because the country's circumstances prevent it from operating.

What makes it worth attempting is the asymmetry: a modest capital requirement (~US$1–2 m to break-even),
an asset that cannot be bought (verified transaction history), and a market of 12–15 million
smallholders with no functioning digital infrastructure.
