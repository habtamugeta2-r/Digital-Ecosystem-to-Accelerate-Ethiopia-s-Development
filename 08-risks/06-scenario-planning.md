# 08.6 · Scenario Planning: Four Futures

*Part of [08 · Risks](README.md)*

Four scenarios to 2032 with distinct strategic implications. Each is assigned a judgemental probability
(⚪). The purpose is not prediction but **preparedness**.

---

## Scenario matrix

| | **Stable & enabling** | **Volatile** |
|---|---|---|
| **State partners** | 🟢 **Scenario A: Rails Open** | 🟠 **Scenario C: Fragmented State** |
| **State competes/restricts** | 🟡 **Scenario B: State Platform** | 🔴 **Scenario D: Rolling Crisis** |

---

## Scenario A — "Rails Open" (probability ~35%)

**The world:** Fayda reaches 100m+; telebirr and M-Pesa interoperate fully; Digital Ethiopia 2030 is
implemented; the state invites private agri platforms as delivery partners; the security situation
stabilises in Oromia and parts of Amhara; the birr stabilises after a period of adjustment; DFI capital
remains available for agri-tech.

| Implication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Fayda verification is cheap, fast and universal |
| **Payments** | Instant, interoperable, low cost |
| **Market** | Farmers adopt quickly as rails improve |
| **Government** | Programme contracts available and paid on time |
| **Capital** | Series A/B achievable at reasonable valuations |
| **Timeline** | Phase 3 by year 4; ecosystem by year 7 |
| **Outcome** | National agricultural infrastructure; ETB 2–3 bn revenue by 2032 |

**Strategy in Scenario A: move fast and integrate deeply.** Being the first application on the rails is
worth more than being the best — because the rails make adoption easy and the data moat compounds.

---

## Scenario B — "State Platform" (probability ~20%)

**The world:** A ministry or a state-owned enterprise launches a national agricultural platform with
mandate, budget and telecom integration. Private platforms are invited to be suppliers or are
out-competed on price (free) but not on capability.

| Implication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Market** | Private platforms squeezed on matching and price |
| **Opportunity** | Becoming the technology provider or the finance layer |
| **Risk** | Being disintermediated from farmer relationships |
| **Timeline** | Decision point in years 2–3 |

**Strategy in Scenario B:**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Compete head-on | ❌ Loses — the state can subsidise indefinitely |
| Become a supplier (build/operate) | ✅ Sustainable but low margin |
| Become the finance layer (state platform + private credit) | ✅ **Best option** — the state rarely wants to lend |
| Become the traceability/compliance layer | ✅ Export-oriented, sticky |
| Exit / sell to the state | ✅ Legitimate outcome for investors |

**The key insight:** even a dominant state platform will not want to (a) originate credit, (b) take
credit risk, or (c) manage exporter traceability. Those remain private opportunities.

## Scenario C — "Fragmented State" (probability ~25%)

**The world:** Federal–regional tension; policy inconsistency; the digital agenda proceeds unevenly;
Fayda coverage stalls in some regions; payments remain fragmented; security is regionally variable;
government payment delays are chronic.

| Implication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Coverage good in cities, patchy in rural areas; fallback essential |
| **Payments** | Multiple rails, inconsistent settlement, high fees |
| **Market** | Regional rather than national platforms |
| **Government** | Contracts exist but are slow and political |
| **Capital** | Harder; DFIs more cautious |

**Strategy in Scenario C:**

| Action | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Build regional depth, not national breadth | Political risk is localised |
| Operate in the 3 most stable regions | Oromia, Addis, Sidama/SNNPR |
| Reduce dependence on government contracts (<25% of revenue) | Payment risk |
| Focus on finance (private lenders) over programmes | Less political |
| Keep the organisation small and flexible | Survive volatility |
| Expect a 10-year rather than 7-year timeline | Patience |

---

## Scenario D — "Rolling Crisis" (probability ~20%)

**The world:** Expanded conflict; prolonged internet shutdowns; banking disruption; sharp currency
collapse; humanitarian emergency; international disengagement; state control of key markets.

| Implication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Operations** | Core regions inaccessible; staff at risk |
| **Payments** | Cash-based economy returns; digital rails unreliable |
| **Market** | Formal trade shrinks; humanitarian supply dominates |
| **Capital** | Unavailable; investors exit |
| **Survival** | 12–24 months of runway |

**Strategy in Scenario D:**

| Action | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Suspend commercial operations in affected regions | Safety first |
| Pivot to **humanitarian delivery** contracts | Cash and voucher assistance continues in crises |
| Reduce to a core team of 10–15 | Preserve runway |
| Protect the data and the entity | The asset survives |
| Maintain relationships (cooperatives, buyers outside conflict zones) | Restart capability |
| Do not raise new equity at distressed valuations | Wait |

**The humanitarian pivot deserves emphasis:** in a crisis, cash and voucher assistance (CVA) becomes the
dominant modality, and platforms that can deliver verified payments to affected populations are in
demand. WFP, UNHCR, and NGOs need exactly the identity + payments + last-mile capability the platform
has built. **This is the survival business model in Scenario D.**

## Probability-weighted outcome

| Scenario | Probability | Year-7 GMV | Year-7 revenue | Enterprise value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: Rails Open | 35% | ETB 100 bn | ETB 3.0 bn | ETB 15–25 bn |
| B: State Platform | 20% | ETB 30 bn | ETB 0.8 bn | ETB 3–6 bn |
| C: Fragmented | 25% | ETB 25 bn | ETB 0.7 bn | ETB 2–5 bn |
| D: Rolling Crisis | 20% | ETB 3 bn | ETB 0.15 bn | ETB 0–1 bn |
| **Expected value** | | **ETB 41 bn** | **ETB 1.24 bn** | **ETB 6–9 bn** |

**Against a peak funding requirement of ~ETB 80–150 m, the expected value is strongly positive** — but
the distribution is wide, and 20% of the time the outcome is near zero.

## Early warning indicators by scenario

| Indicator | Points to |
|---|---|
| Fayda registrations >80 m and rising | A |
| Government announces a national agri platform | B |
| Rising regional tension; security incidents | C or D |
| Internet shutdowns >3 per year | C or D |
| Banking disruption in a core region | D |
| DFI commitments to Ethiopia falling | C or D |
| Peace agreement in Amhara/Tigray | A |
| NBE clarifies escrow and lending rules favourably | A |
| Commodity export bans | C |
| Currency collapse >50% | D |

**Review the scenario assessment every six months** and adjust the regional footprint and cost base
accordingly.

---

## The strategic constant across all scenarios

Whatever happens, four things remain valuable:

1. **A verified ledger of real transactions** — useful in every scenario, including a humanitarian one.
2. **Relationships with cooperatives and buyers** — the only durable distribution.
3. **A small, low-cost, flexible organisation** — survives volatility.
4. **The ability to suspend and restart** — feature flags, light assets, preserved data.

**The platform's strategy should be robust across scenarios, not optimal for one.** That is why it is
agent-anchored, offline-first, regionally diversified, and asset-light — not because those are
efficient, but because they are **survivable**.
