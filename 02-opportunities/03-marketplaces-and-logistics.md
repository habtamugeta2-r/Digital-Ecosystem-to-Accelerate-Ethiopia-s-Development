# 02.3 · Marketplaces and Logistics

*Part of [02 · Opportunities](README.md) · E-commerce ranked #2, logistics #3*

> **Verdict.** Urban and SME e-commerce is the most *fashionable* opportunity and one of the weakest
> in Ethiopia today. Logistics is stronger on unit economics but depends on volume that does not yet
> exist. Both are best built **as modules of the agriculture platform**, where the volume is.

> ⚠️ **A caution about market-size reports.** Several published estimates put Ethiopian e-commerce
> at tens of billions of dollars (e.g. US$29.8 bn in 2025). These are implausible for a country where
> 1% of adults have bought anything online. They typically conflate B2B wholesale, informal trade, and
> total retail turnover. **Use the Findex behavioural data, not the market-research headline numbers.**
> Realistic online retail revenue is in the low hundreds of millions of dollars at most.

### 1.1 Why e-commerce is hard here specifically

| Constraint | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1% online purchase rate | No habitual digital checkout; cash-on-delivery dominates |
| Low purchasing power | Average order values too small to absorb delivery cost |
| Address system | No reliable street addressing outside central Addis Ababa |
| Logistics | Fragmented last-mile; ~13% paved roads nationally |
| Trust | Fraud fear; preference for seeing goods before paying |
| Returns | Cash-on-delivery increases refusal rates and return-to-sender costs |
| Payments | Card penetration minimal; wallet adoption shallow |
| Competition from informal retail | Dense, cheap, immediate — hard to beat on convenience |
| FX on imported goods | Inventory risk and price instability for imported SKUs |

### 1.2 The Jumia lesson, applied

Jumia's trajectory is the most instructive data point on the continent: 11 consecutive years of
losses, exit from South Africa and Tunisia (2024), exit from food delivery (2023), exit from everyday
groceries, a 20% workforce reduction, and a repeated narrowing to core e-commerce. Two lessons:

1. **Control the logistics or the economics never work.** Every survivor builds its own delivery
   layer.
2. **Do not chase GMV.** Chasing revenue at negative contribution margin is how the category dies.

### 1.3 Where urban e-commerce *does* work in Ethiopia

Narrow segments with high value density or urgent demand:
- Restaurant food delivery in Addis (established, repeat-purchase)
- Pharmacy and health products
- Electronics and accessories (23% of e-commerce revenue in one estimate)
- B2B wholesale to informal retailers (the "Buy2Go" model — 31,000 retailers, 25 trucks)
- Fresh produce for urban households, if sourced through a cooperative with cold-chain discipline

**Strategic conclusion:** urban e-commerce is a Phase 3 expansion, entered only when the agricultural
platform already operates logistics and payments in the same cities. Building it standalone in
Phase 1 is capital destruction.

## 2. Logistics: the stronger half

### 2.1 The problem

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Paved roads | ~13% (SSA average ~25%) |
| Road density | 148 km / 1,000 km² (Africa ~204) |
| Roads in good condition | ~26% |
| Trade through Djibouti | 90–95% |
| Cost, Djibouti→Addis per FEU | US$3,555 truck / US$2,462 rail |
| Empty backhaul rate | "almost all trucks return empty" (import-heavy corridor) |
| Urban congestion cost | ~3% of GDP |
| Post-harvest loss attributable to transport/handling | a major share of the 15–25% cereal loss |

### 2.2 What a logistics platform can actually fix

The genuinely addressable inefficiencies are **matching and information**, not the roads:

| Inefficiency | Digital fix | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Empty backhauls | Load board matching return trips | Up to ~40% cost reduction on the backhaul leg |
| Small, unconsolidated loads | Aggregation at cooperative collection points | Lower cost per tonne |
| Long waiting at loading/unloading | Scheduling and slot booking | Faster turnaround, lower demurrage |
| Cash payment to drivers | In-platform freight payment | Removes theft/robbery risk and reconciliation cost |
| No proof of delivery | Photo + geotag + OTP | Reduces disputes and claims |
| Opaque pricing | Published lane rates | Better negotiation for small shippers |
| Cold chain gaps | Temperature-logged carriers (where available) | Directly reduces horticulture loss |

### 2.3 Build sequence (deliberately non-technical first)

| Stage | Product | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | A phone number at each cooperative, a driver database, SMS/WhatsApp broadcast | ~zero |
| 1 | Simple web/app load board with lane rates and bookings | low |
| 2 | Driver app with trip acceptance, tracking (GPS), POD | medium |
| 3 | Integration: freight payment, insurance, fuel cards, telematics | medium |
| 4 | Cold chain and specialised equipment marketplace | higher |

**The critical insight:** a "logistics platform" in Ethiopia in 2026 is a **dispatch desk with
software**, not an Uber. Driver smartphone penetration, literacy and trust do not yet support
app-only matching. Asset-light brokering with strong operations is the model.

### 2.4 Last-mile delivery

Urban last-mile is already contested by Eshi Express, Tikus, Zmall and others, operating in a dense
but thin-margin market. Rural last-mile has no meaningful competitor — because it is hard. The
practical answer is **the cooperative and the agent as the last mile**: goods move to a cooperative
collection point by truck, and the farmer collects. This is unglamorous and correct.

## 3. Why these belong inside the agriculture platform

| Capability | Standalone e-commerce needs it | Agri platform already has it |
|---|---|---|
| Payment rails + escrow | Build | Built for agri |
| Verified identity | Build | Fayda integration |
| Buyer / seller trust | Build from zero | Cooperative anchoring |
| Volume | Acquire expensively | 12 m household base |
| Delivery density | Build | Cooperative collection points |
| Regulatory position | Full | Shared |
| Revenue per transaction | Small (retail) | Large (bulk, B2B) |

**A bulk grain movement of 30 tonnes at ETB 25,000/t is ETB 750,000 of GMV in one transaction.** You
would need ~1,500 urban consumer orders at ETB 500 to match it. In a low-income, low-digital-
penetration market, **B2B and bulk B2C logistics is where the arithmetic works.**

## Key takeaways

1. Ethiopian e-commerce is real but tiny: **1% of adults have bought online with a digital payment**,
   and 58% of e-commerce startups earn under US$10k/year.
2. Ignore the multi-billion-dollar market-research estimates; they are not credible for this market.
3. Jumia's history is the governing lesson: control logistics, refuse negative-margin GMV, focus.
4. **Logistics** is the stronger standalone opportunity, but its addressable inefficiencies are
   **matching and information**, not road quality.
5. Both are Phase 3 modules of the agriculture platform, not Phase 1 standalone businesses.
6. Design the first logistics product as a **dispatch desk with software**, not an app marketplace.
