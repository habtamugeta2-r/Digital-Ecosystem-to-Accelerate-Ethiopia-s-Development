# 04.6 · Market Size and Competition

*Part of [04 · Constraints](README.md)*

> **The constraint in one line.** The formal market is **small, thin and cheap**, and the most powerful
> competitor is the state. The model must therefore be **basis points on large volume**, not margin
> per customer.

---

## 1. Sizing the addressable market honestly

### 1.1 Macro context

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Nominal GDP (rebased) | ETB 31.86 trn ≈ US$210.7 bn (FY2025/26) |
| GDP per capita | ~US$1,300–1,560 |
| Population below US$3/day | 43% |
| Agriculture share of GDP | ~31.6–32.8% |
| Agricultural value added | ~US$41.5–47.7 bn |
| Cereal production | 30–33 million tonnes |
| Coffee export value | US$2.653 bn (FY2024/25) |
| Adults with a financial account | 49% |
| Adults buying online digitally | 1% |

### 1.2 TAM / SAM / SOM for an agriculture marketplace

All figures **illustrative (⚪)**, derived from the sourced inputs above.

| Layer | Definition | Volume | Value at ~ETB 25,000/t |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | All cereal, pulse and oilseed production | 30–33 Mt cereals + ~3 Mt pulses/oilseeds | ~ETB 825–900 bn (~US$5.1–5.6 bn) |
| **SAM** | Surplus-producing woredas with cooperative presence and road access — roughly 40% of production | ~13–15 Mt | ~ETB 340–380 bn |
| **SOM (Year 3)** | Realistic capture with 3–5 regions, 3 commodities, cooperative anchors | 1–2% of TAM | **ETB 8–18 bn GMV** |
| **SOM (Year 5)** | With finance, logistics and multiple verticals | 3–5% of TAM | **ETB 25–45 bn GMV** |

**Revenue at 1–3% take:** Year 3 ~ETB 80–540 m; Year 5 ~ETB 250 m–1.35 bn. Plus finance, inputs,
logistics and programme revenue.

**Interpretation:** this is a **viable business at Year 5 scale but not a venture-scale
hyper-growth story on marketplace fees alone**. The finance and programme lines carry the economics.
That is why the business model section has six revenue streams, not one.

> ⚠️ **Do not use third-party market-research figures for Ethiopian e-commerce.** Published estimates
> in the tens of billions of dollars are not consistent with the Findex data (1% of adults buying
> online). They conflate total retail, wholesale and informal trade.

## 2. The competitive map

### Tier 1 — State and state-linked (cooperate, don't compete)

| Player | Position | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethio Telecom / telebirr** | 60.6 m users; wallet, USSD, agents, micro-loans | Payment rail partner. Could build an agri marketplace; if it does, become its application layer |
| **Safaricom / M-Pesa** | 5.2 m active; interoperability; 15 m subscribers | Payment rail and possible co-investor |
| **EthSwitch** | Switch, instant payments, EthQR, UGPP | Settlement partner; potential API provider |
| **Commercial Bank of Ethiopia & state banks** | ~half of banking assets; large branch network | Credit partner; distribution partner |
| **Ethiopia Commodity Exchange** | Grading, warehousing, online trading, e-receipts | Partner for grading/storage; possible co-marketplace |
| **ATI / MoA** | 8028, 6077, Digital Market Linkage Platform, Input Voucher System | Content, channel and programme partner |

### Tier 2 — Private platforms (compete or partner)

| Player | Position | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Lersha** | Phygital agri platform; 4,000+ agents; 400,000+ farmers; IFC-backed; inputs, mechanisation, advisory, credit | Closest competitor. Partner, differentiate on marketplace/escrow, or acquire |
| **Shega, Zmall, Buy2Go, Store 251** | E-commerce and delivery | Potential urban logistics partners in Phase 3 |
| **Eshi Express, Tikus** | Last-mile delivery | Contract partners |
| **Kacha, Arifpay, Chapa, SantimPay** | Payment gateways and fintech | Payment integration partners |
| **Banks' agri desks** | Credit and value-chain programmes | Credit partners |

### Tier 3 — Informal (the real incumbent)

| Player | Position | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Village traders / aggregators | Provide credit, transport, immediate cash, emergency support | **The actual competitor.** Must be out-bundled, not out-priced |
| Cooperatives (weak ones) | Aggregation, but poor records and governance | Digitise them; make them the partner |
| Brokers (*delala*) | Match buyers and sellers for a fee | Recruit as platform agents; they have the relationships |

**The most important competitive insight:** the incumbent is not another startup. It is a **bundled
relationship of credit + logistics + immediacy + trust** provided by a local trader. Competing on price
alone fails. Competing on **bundle** is the only viable strategy.

## 3. Why the state is both the biggest risk and the biggest opportunity

| Scenario | Probability | Impact | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| State builds its own agri marketplace and prefers it | Medium | High | Become the implementation partner (PPPs are the Ethiopian pattern) |
| telebirr adds agri features incrementally | High | Medium | Integrate deeply; be the best agri experience on telebirr |
| Regulatory preference for state payment channels | Medium | Medium | Multi-rail architecture |
| Government mandates platform use for subsidies | Low–Medium | Very high (positive) | Position as a programme partner from Phase 1 |
| State expropriates or duplicates a successful platform | Low | Very high | Cooperative equity, distributed data, transparent governance |

**Posture:** be **inside** the state's programme, not beside it. The DAR 2032 has a PMU and no
implementation capacity; the NAFIR has credit lines and no origination channel; the NDPS has G2P
targets and no last-mile. Those are three open doors.

## 4. The "small market" fallacy and the "small formal market" reality

Ethiopia is not a small market in people (135.5 m) or production (30–33 Mt of cereals). It is small in
**formal, monetisable, digital transaction value**. The distinction matters:

| If you think… | You will… |
|---|---|
| "135 million people is a huge market" | Build a consumer app and fail (1% online purchase rate) |
| "The formal market is tiny" | Under-invest and miss the largest aggregation opportunity in Africa |
| "Agriculture is 32% of a US$210 bn economy" | Correctly size a B2B transaction business at ETB hundreds of billions of GMV |

**The correct framing:** a **large, low-monetisation market** where value is captured through
**volume × basis points × bundling**, not through high-margin consumer products.

## 5. Pricing constraints

| Constraint | Implication |
|---|---|
| Farm-gate margins are thin (often <20% of revenue) | Fees must be ≤3%, ideally 1–2% |
| Intermediary spreads are 10–30% | There is room to undercut substantially and still earn |
| Government programmes pay per-beneficiary, not per-transaction | Programme revenue can exceed marketplace revenue early |
| Donors pay for outcomes and M&E | Build measurement into the product from day one |
| Agents need ≥US$70–100/month to be active | Commission design must clear this bar per agent |
| Farmers will not pay for advice | Advice is a customer-acquisition cost, not a revenue line |

## 6. Geographic prioritisation

| Region | Agricultural importance | Security | Connectivity | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oromia** | Largest producer; coffee, cereals; 19.2 m Fayda registrations | Mixed (conflict in some zones) | Good in central areas | **1 (pilot)** |
| **Amhara** | Major cereals; 8.3 m Fayda | Conflict-affected in parts | Moderate | 2 (with caution) |
| **SNNPR / South Ethiopia** | Coffee, enset, diverse | Moderate | Moderate | 2 |
| **Sidama** | Coffee | Moderate | Moderate | 2 |
| **Tigray** | Cereals, sesame | High risk (renewed conflict 2026) | Degraded | 4 (post-conflict recovery) |
| **Somali** | Livestock, sesame | Drought, security | Low | 4 (pastoralist-specific product) |
| **Afar** | Livestock, salt | Drought, security | Low | 4 |
| **Addis Ababa** | Consumption market, processors | Stable | Best | 1 (buyer side) |
| **Central / SW Ethiopia** | Diverse | Moderate | Moderate | 2–3 |

**Recommended pilot:** a **surplus woreda cluster in Oromia** (e.g. Arsi/Bale for wheat, or East/West
Shewa for teff/maize) with a buyer base in Addis Ababa. Rationale: highest production, cooperative
presence, road access to Addis, good Fayda coverage, and manageable security risk relative to Amhara
and Tigray.

**Explicit non-recommendation:** do not pilot in Tigray, Amhara (conflict zones) or the lowland
pastoral regions in Phase 1.

## Decisions forced

| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Market framing | Large volume, low monetisation → basis points and bundling |
| First region | Oromia surplus cluster + Addis buyers |
| Fee ceiling | 3% maximum; target 1–2% |
| Competitive posture | Partner with state rails and ATI; differentiate from Lersha on marketplace and escrow; out-bundle the informal trader |
| Advice pricing | Free (acquisition cost) |
| Agent viability | ≥US$70–100/month bundled income |
| Geographic exclusions | No Tigray/Amhara-conflict/pastoral pilots in Phase 1 |
